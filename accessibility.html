Accessibility

This site claims WCAG 2.1 AA as a professional standard, so it should say plainly what that means here, what was tested, and what is not yet right.

Conformance target

This site aims to meet WCAG 2.1 level AA. That is the standard I hold production work to, and the one the component libraries described on the portfolio were built against.

What that means in practice

  • Every interactive control is reachable and operable by keyboard alone, in a reading order that matches the visual one.
  • Focus is always visible, and never trapped. Opening a disclosure moves focus to what opened; closing it returns focus to the control that opened it.
  • Text contrast is at least 4.5:1 in both the dark and light themes, measured against the surface actually painted behind each element rather than assumed from the token.
  • Content is structured with real landmarks and headings — one h1, no skipped levels, and a named region per section — so a screen reader can navigate by structure.
  • All motion is decorative, driven by transform and opacity, and switches itself off under prefers-reduced-motion. The auto-advancing carousel has a visible pause control, stops on hover and on focus, and is replaced by a static grid when reduced motion is requested.
  • Images carry text alternatives; decorative graphics are hidden from assistive technology; the animated role line in the header is hidden from it and the full text exposed instead.
  • The page works without JavaScript: content is in the markup, not assembled at runtime.

How it was tested

  • Keyboard-only traversal of every control on the page.
  • Programmatic contrast measurement of every text style against the surface actually painted behind it, in both themes. The lowest ratio on the page is 6.0:1, against a 4.5:1 requirement.
  • Structural checks: landmark names, heading order, accessible names on every link and button, no duplicate IDs, and no focusable duplicates inside the carousel.
  • Layout verified from 375 px upward with no horizontal scrolling.
  • Manual review alongside this — automated checks catch roughly a third of real barriers, so they are a floor, not a verdict.

Known limitations

  • The downloadable résumé is a PDF exported from Word. Its tagging has not been audited, so it may not meet the same standard as this page. If you need the résumé in an accessible format, ask and I will send one.
  • The work carousel can be dragged with a pointer. Dragging has no keyboard equivalent, though the previous, pause and next buttons cover the same ground.
  • The current round of testing was keyboard, contrast and structure. A full pass with VoiceOver and NVDA — the screen readers I test production work with — is still outstanding for this site specifically.
  • This site has not been audited by an independent third party. Everything above is my own testing.

Reporting a problem

If something here fails you — an unreachable control, an unreadable contrast, a screen reader reading nonsense — please tell me. Email yaseerhaja@gmail.com with the page, the assistive technology and browser you were using, and what happened. I will reply within five working days and will tell you either when it is fixed or why it is not.
